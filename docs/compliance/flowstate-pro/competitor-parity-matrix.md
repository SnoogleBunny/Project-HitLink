## 1. How to read this benchmark

This matrix benchmarks clinic journeys relevant to Flowstate Pro. It does **not** set a requirement to reproduce Jane's full practice-management catalog or Epic's enterprise catalog. Flowstate decisions and legal/control gates take precedence over competitor evidence.

### Evidence and scope labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `VERIFIED` | A first-party operational guide, pricing matrix, public technical specification, or similarly concrete page documents the capability. This verifies the public statement, not tenant-level behavior. |
| `MARKETING_CLAIM` | A first-party product page describes a capability or outcome without enough public implementation detail to treat it as an operational specification. |
| `MODULE_OR_ADD_ON` | Separate module, paid add-on, tier-gated feature, partner product, transaction-priced service, or customer-specific deployment choice. |
| `REGION_DEPENDENT` | Availability, workflow, processing, payer support, or pricing varies by country/region. |
| `MANUAL_OR_PARTIAL` | First-party material documents a manual, support-mediated, externally completed, limited, early-access, or otherwise incomplete workflow. |
| `UNCLEAR` | Public first-party evidence is absent, contradictory, login-protected, or insufficient to establish current general availability. |
| `OUT_OF_SCOPE` | Intentionally not part of the approved Flowstate Pro target. It is not a product defect. |

A row may carry more than one label. `VERIFIED` means the public documentation is concrete; it does not mean Flowstate independently exercised the feature or verified a vendor's security/compliance assertion.

### Flowstate treatment

- **V1** — part of the approved Pro-US minimum safe clinical journey in Q189–Q193.
- **LATER** — approved later roadmap domain in Q76–Q87 or a parity expansion requiring its own evidence and activation gate.
- **OUT_OF_SCOPE** — not part of the approved clinic product target; integrate with external systems where applicable rather than rebuild their core.
- **BLOCKED** — direction exists, but vendor, counsel, policy, profile, contracting, or production approval remains unresolved.

### Jane gaps worth carrying into design review

1. Open standards/API access is a clear differentiation opportunity.
2. Custody-critical export should be self-service, reconciled, and evidence-producing rather than support-mediated.
3. Migration needs delta/cutover controls and truthful handling of unsupported financial history.
4. Medical workflows need deeper prescribing, lab, allergy, medication-safety, and provenance support than Jane's current public materials establish.
5. Messaging can support care-team/group workflows without inheriting Jane's one-to-one-only limitation, but only after retention and emergency-use controls are approved.
6. Multilingual forms and accessibility evidence should be first-class requirements, not manual workarounds or an undated improvement claim.
7. Regional storage must not be represented as universal regional processing; feature-level transfer paths must remain visible.

### Epic gaps and constraints worth carrying into design review

1. Public product pages are often broad marketing descriptions; detailed implementation evidence is frequently customer-login protected. Do not convert catalog language into a Flowstate requirement without a concrete clinic journey.
2. Epic's interoperability breadth is a useful standards benchmark, but interface directionality, resource version, write support, customer enablement, and module dependencies must remain explicit.
3. EHI export is concrete but manual and configuration-dependent; Flowstate can make routine tenant export and reconciliation easier.
4. Epic's department, payer, hospital, research, and population-health scope is intentionally not a Flowstate parity target.
5. Public evidence did not establish exact Epic hosting/residency, identity, break-glass, audit-retention, or AI-validation controls for the Virginia/BC profiles. Those remain `UNCLEAR`, not presumed absent and not presumed equivalent.
6. Flowstate's credible differentiation is a smaller deployable surface, profile-specific controls, standards-based portability, multilingual/accessibility evidence, and lower implementation burden—not an unsupported claim of equal enterprise breadth.
